Jacob and Israel

Jacob is who we are. Israel is who God makes us.

You get from one to the other the way he did: you stop pretending, you tell God your real name, and you hold on.

Jacob means heel-grabber. He was born holding his brother's heel, and he lived up to it.

He tricked Esau out of the birthright, then dressed up as him to steal their father's blessing. His blind father asked, "Who are you, my son?" and he said, "I am Esau."

He ran for his life. Twenty years later Esau is coming to meet him with 400 men.

  1. The gift to Esau

    "I will cover his face with the gift that goes before my face. Afterward I will see his face. Perhaps he will lift up my face." Genesis 32:20, word for word from the Hebrew

    אֲכַפְּרָה פָנָיו בַּמִּנְחָה הַהֹלֶכֶת לְפָנָי וְאַחֲרֵי־כֵן אֶרְאֶה פָנָיו אוּלַי יִשָּׂא פָנָי

    • Four times in one sentence: his face, my face, his face, my face. Jacob has spent his whole life managing faces.
    • He sends herds of goats, sheep, camels and cattle ahead of him, wave after wave.
    • "Cover" is kipper. It means to cover a wrong so it stops standing between two people. It is the Bible's word for atonement. Jacob is trying to buy his brother's forgiveness.
    • The mercy seat comes from the same word. It was the gold lid on the ark of the covenant. Once a year the high priest sprinkled blood on it to cover Israel's sins, and God said, "There I will meet with you."
    • Paul says God put Jesus forward as our mercy seat (Romans 3:25). Jacob tried to pay for his own covering. God gave His Son for ours.

  2. The wrestling

    "I will not let you go unless you bless me." So he said to him, "What is your name?" And he said, "Jacob." He said, "Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel." Genesis 32:26–28
    • It happens at the Jabbok River, and in Hebrew the words sound alike: Jacob is Ya'akov יַעֲקֹב, the river is Yabbok יַבֹּק, and "wrestled" is vaye'avek וַיֵּאָבֵק. The place and the fight both sound like his name.
    • A man wrestles him until dawn and throws his hip out of joint. He keeps holding.
    • The last time someone asked who he was, he lied. Tonight he gives his real name, and God gives him a new one.
    • Isaiah talks to the whole nation with both names, over and over: "Why do you say, O Jacob, and speak, O Israel, 'My way is hidden from the LORD'?" (Isaiah 40:27)
    • And: "Thus says the LORD, your Creator, O Jacob, and He who formed you, O Israel: Do not fear, for I have redeemed you; I have called you by name; you are Mine." (Isaiah 43:1)

  3. What Hosea adds

    "Yes, he wrestled with the angel and prevailed; he wept and sought His favor." Hosea 12:4
    • Genesis never says he cried. Hosea does.
    • The wrestler was crying and begging for grace the whole time he held on.

  4. Face to face

    "I have seen God face to face, yet my life has been preserved." Genesis 32:30
    • He names the place Peniel, "face of God," and limps away as the sun comes up.
    • Centuries later Moses asks to see God's glory. God says, "No man can see Me and live," and hides him in the cleft of the rock.
    • Then God passes by and speaks His own name: "The LORD, the LORD God, compassionate and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in lovingkindness and truth" (Exodus 34:6). At the river Jacob asked for God's name and was told, "Why is it that you ask my name?" (Genesis 32:29). Moses heard it.
    • In the Song of Songs, the one hiding in the cleft of the rock is the beloved, and the Lover says to her, "Let me see your face."

  5. Esau forgives Jacob

    "Then Esau ran to meet him and embraced him, and fell on his neck and kissed him, and they wept." Genesis 33:4
    • Jesus gives the father of the prodigal son the same three moves: he ran, fell on his neck and kissed him (Luke 15:20). Most English Bibles soften it to "embraced him," but the Greek says "fell on his neck," the same as Genesis.
    • Jacob tells Esau, "I see your face as one sees the face of God."
    • Then he says, "Please take my blessing." Twenty years after he stole Esau's blessing, he finally gives it back.

  6. The priests' blessing

    Before the river, Jacob hoped Esau would accept him. The Hebrew says, "Perhaps he will lift up my face."

    אוּלַי יִשָּׂא פָנָי

    ulai yissa fanai, Genesis 32:20

    Later God gave Moses the words the priests were to speak over Israel: "This is how you shall bless the sons of Israel" (Numbers 6:23).

    The priests spoke it over the whole nation at once, but every "you" in the Hebrew is singular. It is said to everyone and to each one.

    It is also the oldest piece of Scripture ever found. Two tiny silver scrolls from about 600 BC, dug up in Jerusalem in 1979, carry this blessing.

    "The LORD bless you, and keep you; the LORD make His face shine on you, and be gracious to you; the LORD lift up His countenance on you, and give you peace." Numbers 6:24–26

    יִשָּׂא יְהוָה פָּנָיו אֵלֶיךָ

    yissa Adonai panav elekha, Numbers 6:26"Lift up His countenance" is Jacob's phrase: lift up the face. What Jacob hoped for from his brother, God promised to His people.

    Every line of the blessing already happened to Jacob.

For us

We all start out like Jacob. At Bethel, running from home, he makes God a deal: "If God will be with me and keep me… and give me food to eat and clothes to wear… then the LORD will be my God" (Genesis 28:20–21). Me first.

The Song of Songs grows up the same way:

  • "My beloved is mine, and I am his." (2:16) Mine comes first.
  • "I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine." (6:3) His comes first.
  • "I am my beloved's, and his desire is for me." (7:10) Only His.

Twenty years after Bethel, Jacob is in the dark at the river, crying and holding on. The Song says it too: "I held on to him and would not let him go." (3:4)

He walks away with God's name on him and gives back the blessing he stole. Jacob's whole life is that love story, from wanting what God can give him to becoming His. It is our story too.

The last chapter of the Bible says of God's people: "They will see His face, and His name will be on their foreheads."
