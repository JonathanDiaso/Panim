## 1. Why the last set came back wrong

The old sheet was **90% camera and light, 10% world.** Count the words in the old chapter X
prompt: *85mm, eye level, close, direct bright light with no haze, sharp lashes, plain warm
background* — eleven clauses of optics, and for the human being at the centre of the
picture, four words: **"a woman's face."**

A generative model fills silence with its median. Its median woman is a 2020s studio
portrait: styled brows, foundation, a blow-dry, straight orthodontic teeth, a bare neck.
That is not the model failing. That is the prompt never saying otherwise.

**Three failures, all the same failure:**

| | what the prompt said | what came back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **No period on people** | "a woman's face", "a man's profile", "sailors" | contemporary faces, contemporary grooming |
| **No period on objects** | "a wooden merchant ship", "clay lamps", "a low chair" | a Viking longship, generic pottery, a dining stool |
| **No specificity on the thing the chapter is about** | "a colossal woven temple curtain" | a curtain with no cherubim, in a Gothic cathedral |

The last one is the worst, because the curtain **is the chapter**. Chapter VIII spends four
paragraphs on what was woven into that cloth — *cherubim*, Eden's guards, embroidered on the
barrier for a thousand years — and the prompt asked for a blank curtain. The picture could
not carry the idea because the idea was never in the prompt. **It came back blank, and in a
cathedral.**

**The fix is not more adjectives.** It is naming, once and flatly: the century, the place,
who these people are, what they are wearing and how it was made, what the object is made of.
Then the optics, which were always fine.

### Two words to never use

- **"biblical"** — pulls Renaissance and Victorian religious painting. Halos, blue-and-white
  Madonnas, blond Christs, Doré engravings. Say the century and the place instead.
- **"ancient"** — pulls Greco-Roman. Fluted marble columns, white togas, laurel. Almost
  nothing in this book is Greco-Roman. Say *Iron Age Judah*, *Late Bronze Age Levant*,
  *Second Temple Judea, first century.*

Also avoid **"photorealistic"** and **"not an illustration"** — both flatten the light. And
never stack adjectives: say the light source, its direction and its colour **once**, say what
things are made of, and say in one clause what the picture is about.

Six of eleven pictures in the first set were ordered in identical light (*golden hour, god
rays, warm amber*) which is why the set came back samey. The site's paper runs **night to
morning** across the ten chapters. If every plate is golden, the arc dies in the pictures.

**Constant: the optics.** Backlight. Real haze. Blacks that stay black. Depth in layers.

## 4. How to write the second prompt

**Subtract.** Darker, emptier, closer, further back. Name one thing to remove and one thing
to keep. **Never add a new element in 2** — you get a new picture instead of a better one.

Four words that do most of the work: *darker · emptier · closer · further back.*

## 6. Say the positive. Negation is the weakest instruction you can give.

🔁 **Expanded 2026-08-28 from six delivered frames, not from theory.**

Image models are poor at negation — naming a thing puts it in the model's head whether or not
the word "no" stands in front of it. The last sheet had two examples. Grading all fourteen
files produced **six**:

| slot | the prompt forbade, by name | what came back |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `ch06-shine` | no bokeh orbs | bokeh orbs, throughout |
| `ch02-trees` | no light shafts added for effect | god rays through the trees |
| `ch02-trees` | no European faces | long wavy auburn hair |
| `ch02-trees` | no ring unless named | a ring on her finger |
| `ch09-charcoal` | no flames — coals only | open orange flames |
| `ch09-emmaus` | no chairs, no benches | a wooden stool |

Six instructions, all written down, all ignored — and several arguably *summoned* by being
named. This is not the model being disobedient; it is a known property of how these systems
read text.

**The fix is not to delete the never-list.** It stays, because it is the checklist you grade
the delivered frame against, and a few models do honour it. Two things change:

1. **Every constraint that matters must also exist as a positive statement in the body**,
   where it does work.
2. **The never-list is now short and per-slot.** A twelve-clause boilerplate repeated on every
   prompt is twelve chances to summon something, most of them irrelevant to the frame. Three
   to five, chosen for *this* picture, is the whole list.

| instead of | say |
| --- | --- |
| no lens flare, no god rays | *the only light comes from the fire; everything the fire does not reach is black* |
| no bokeh, no dust sparkles | *the air is clear and still; the background is a flat wall of unlit foliage* |
| no European faces | *deep olive-brown skin weathered by sun, black hair, dark eyes, strong brows* — she is already described, so there is no gap for the median to fill |
| not a hood, not a cowl | *one flat unhemmed square of cloth laid over the face, its four corners visible, two tucked at the temples* |
| no chairs | *a woven reed floor mat and a bolster cushion, the hollow of someone still in it* |
| no flames | *matte black lumps of burnt wood glowing deep red in the cracks, and nothing standing above them* |
| no masonry | *bare earth and roots underfoot; nothing in the frame has been cut, stacked or shaped by a tool* |

The rule in one line: **a silence is filled with the median. A description is not.**
Every failure in §1 was a silence. The six above were a silence with a "no" standing next to
it, which is the same silence.

## 7. What is in every block below

So a new slot can be written to match. In this order, one paragraph, no headings:

1. **The subject and the place**, in one sentence, with the century named.
2. **Period, casting and dress** — trimmed to what is visible. No face description in a frame
   where the face is turned away.
3. **The material facts** — what the objects are made of and how they were manufactured.
4. **The light**, once: source, direction, colour. From the arc in §3.
5. **The optics** — focal length, height, where the subject sits in the frame.
6. **One clause saying what the picture is about.**
7. **Three to five nevers**, chosen for this frame only.

### `ch01-scroll` — the oldest words · ✅ **replaced round six — script AND both style flags fixed**

The delivered frame has **modern square Hebrew** scratched into the metal, clearly enough to
read as such, under the caption *"the oldest words we have."* Square Hebrew is Aramaic script
and eight hundred years too late. Ketef Hinnom KH1 is **palaeo-Hebrew** — the older angular
alphabet, close to Phoenician: no square blocks, no crowns, no vowel points. Say it as a
positive, describe the stroke, and forbid the square forms by shape rather than by name.

**Prompt 1**

```text
Extreme close-up of a tiny rolled strip of silver foil lying in an open palm. It is about
97mm of sheet wound into a cylinder roughly 11mm across — the size of a cigarette filter, no
bigger — blackened to charcoal grey with tarnish, cracked and flaking at the exposed edge, the
coil visibly loose. Faint letters are scratched into the metal with a hard stylus in
PALAEO-HEBREW: an angular Phoenician-style alphabet made of thin straight scratched strokes
and open triangular heads, spidery and irregular, each letter a few scratches rather than a
drawn block. The letters must NOT be square or rectilinear, must have no thick stems, no
serifs, no crowns and no dots or marks above or below the line. A low raking light catches
only their incised edges. The palm behind is weathered, out of focus, filling the background.
135mm macro, the scroll filling the frame.
Never: no square or block letterforms, no vowel points, no ornament, no chain, no case, no
text legible enough to read as a whole word.
```

**Prompt 2**

```text
Same object, closer. Fill the frame with the silver alone, the palm only a soft field behind
it. Rake the light lower until the scratched letters are the brightest thing in the picture
and everything else is dull grey metal. Keep the strokes thin, angular and irregular.
```

> at the left edge in a pale robe, his hand against her face. **That is close to the best plate in
> the set.**
>
> Three things diverge from the brief that was written for it: it is an **interior** with a stone
> wall and a window, not a garden; there is a **second figure**, where the book's rule is no full
> face until chapter X and the brief said she was alone; and she is **faintly smiling** where the
> brief said steady and not smiling. **None of those is an error — they are three places where
> the picture disagreed with its brief and the picture won.**
>
> **So the prompt below has been rewritten to describe THIS picture**, not the brief it came from.
> That is the whole reason it exists now: a reroll cannot accidentally lose what is good here, and
> the frame can be regenerated at higher resolution later without losing it.

**Prompt 1**

```text
Close portrait of a woman in her late forties, first-century Judea, inside a small house of
rough undressed limestone, lit from a window out of frame to the right. She is Levantine:
deep olive-brown skin weathered by sun, HEAVILY FRECKLED across the nose and both cheeks, deep

of any kind, no modern dentistry. Her dark hair is pulled back flat and almost entirely

with no seam, no fastening and no tailoring. Beneath it a mantle of the same coarse cloth.
She is looking straight out at the camera, eyes open and level. Her face is wet: tracks of
tears down both cheeks, one tear on the cheek, and the eyes still full. Her mouth is closed
```

## How to grade a delivered frame

Open the file. Do not grade from the note about the file — every stale claim corrected on
2026-08-28 came from someone reading a note instead of a picture.

1. **Century check.** Is there anything in the frame that had not been invented yet? Masonry,
   metal, tailoring, glass, a hinge, a chair.
2. **Never-list check.** Read the block's own never line and look for each item. Six of them
   came back anyway last time; that is the number to expect.
3. **Caption check.** Read the caption in `content/images.js` and ask whether this picture can
   carry that sentence. `ch01-scroll` fails this and nothing else.
4. **Arc check.** Compare against §3. Is this chapter's light on the page?
5. **Face rule.** No full face before chapter X.
