Ch. 01

The God Who Sees

Jerusalem, 1979.

On a rocky slope above the Hinnom Valley, an excavation is coming up empty. The tombs here were cut into the hillside more than twenty-five centuries ago, and grave robbers have had nearly all of that time to work. Chamber after chamber gives up the same verdict: dust, shattered pottery, nothing worth the dig.

The archaeologist has one problem left on the site, and the problem is thirteen years old. So the boy gets the assignment every excavation keeps ready for a restless kid. Chamber 25. Sweep it out. Stay out of the way.

Somewhere in that long dead hour the silence gets to you, and you start knocking on the ancient stone floor. No reason. Just to give the quiet something to answer. The stone knocks back hollow.

The LORD make His face shine on you, and be gracious to you.

Numbers 6:24–26 / c. 600 BC / silver

Someone was buried wearing those words. Out of creation, covenant, commandments, an entire thundering library of prophets, what the people of Jerusalem chose to carry into the grave was a prayer that God's face would turn toward them and shine.

Thread ↗ The silver scrolls return in Ch. 10, when the blessing is finally spoken over a face that is no longer hidden.
