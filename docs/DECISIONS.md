## A.3 🛑 MEASURED, AND IT CHANGES THE QUESTION: ONE FIELD FEEDS THREE PLACES

**Before picking a line, know what it costs — this was measured in a real browser, not
estimated.**

**`hook` is a single field in `content/chapters.js`, and it is printed in three places:**

| where | how it is set | budget |
|---|---|---|
| **the ribbon caption** (`.pl-hook`) | **display voice, 32px line-height on a phone**, in a 362px column | 🔴 **the binding constraint** |
| **the contents row** (`.toc-hook`) | small, one or two lines | comfortable |
| **the chapter opening** (`.chapter-hook`) | body size, italic, a 58ch measure | **roomy — 2 lines at 1440** |

🔴 **The ribbon is where a long line hurts, and it hurts on every plate at once.** The
caption reserves height for the tallest hook so the card underneath does not jump under your
thumb on every swipe. **Measured at 402px:**

| | lines at 402 | lines at 1440 |
|---|---|---|
| the live ten | **3–4** (the reserve holds 5) | 2 |
| **VIII-C** and **IX-C** | ✅ **4** | 2 · 3 |
| I-C · IX-A | 6 | 3 · 4 |
| I-B · IV-B · VII-A · VIII-A · IX-B | 7 | 4 |
| I-A · IV-C · VIII-B | 8 | 4–5 |
| **IV-A** | 🔴 **9** | 5 |

**So the honest answer to *"maybe 3 sentences is allowable"* is: not in the ribbon.** Only
**VIII-C** and **IX-C** fit what is reserved today. **Every other alternative above would add
between 32px and 162px of reserved air under all ten plates on a phone** — which is the
exact fault you already flagged tonight (*"it shouldnt take up so much space top to bottom"*).

## A.4 ☐ SO THIS IS THE REAL DECISION, AND IT IS ARCHITECTURAL

* ☐ **A** ⭐ **my pick — split the field.** `hook` stays the short line and keeps feeding
  the ribbon and the contents. A new optional `standfirst` feeds **only the chapter
  opening**, where there is room for three sentences and where the reader has already
  committed. **You get the heart where a reader is standing still, and the ribbon stays
  tight where they are swiping.** Cost: **one line in `tools/build-chapters.py`** (carry it
  across a rebuild the way `hook` already is), **one line in `js/render.js`**, and a rebuild.
  **~45 minutes total, no layout risk anywhere else.**
* ☐ **B** — **Keep one field and take the longer lines everywhere.** Simplest, and it costs

  phone first.**
* ☐ **C** — **Keep one field and only take the two that fit** (VIII-C, IX-C). **Free, ships

* ☐ **D** — **Split it the other way:** the ribbon prints only the *first sentence* of the

⭐ **If you pick A, my picks are I-A, IV-A, VIII-A, IX-B.** ⭐ **If you pick C, take VIII-C

## C.1 ☐ *"Shoudl it say chapt 1 then hebrewbelow that whats the design choice?"*

**What ships now, and the reasoning, so you can overrule it knowing what it was:** the
Hebrew mark sits in the **margin column to the left of the title**, and the numeral —
`CHAPTER IV` — sits on the same baseline row beside it. **On a phone the margin column does
not exist**, so the two share a single row above the title, mark first.

**The design choice being made is that the Hebrew is not a heading.** It is a *mark* — the
thing a reader's eye lands on to know where they are, the way a printed book puts a folio
or an ornament in the margin. **A mark stacked above a numeral above a title is three
headings in a row, and the reader has to descend three steps before they reach a word they
can read.** Beside it, it is one step.

* ☐ **A** ⭐ *my pick, and what ships* — **mark in the margin, numeral beside it, title
  below.**

  hierarchy, more vertical space, and it reads more like a textbook.
* ☐ **C** — **drop the numeral from the opening entirely** and let the Hebrew carry it. The
  contents and the running head both already say which chapter this is. **Boldest, and the
  most like a real book.**
