L1 · nothing reflows, everything answers

The ledger

The list from yesterday, made expensive. The title steps aside and hands its row to the blurb and the plate — so pointing at a chapter gives you the picture and the sentence, in one gesture, with the row never changing height. The runtime is drawn as a length, because 22 min and 59 min look identical as text.

ReadsTitle always. Blurb and plate arrive on point; the row never changes height. ClickGoes to the chapter. One target, one meaning. PhoneBlurb sits permanently under the title; no plate.

L2 · your back-cover idea, and it replaces the contents

The back cover

Point and the plate peeks. Click and the row opens into a jacket back — the plate at reading size, the blurb at reading size, the scripture the chapter opens on, and the two things you can do about it. One open at a time, like an accordion, so the page never becomes a wall. This is the one that can replace the contents section outright, because it carries everything the contents carried and more.

ReadsTen quiet lines. Nothing loud until asked. ClickOpens the jacket. Going to the chapter is a named button inside it. PhoneWorks unchanged — tap opens, the panel stacks.

L3 · the flip you asked for, built honestly

The turn

One click turns the plate over onto its back, where the blurb is printed on the same paper stock as the page. The back carries a named way out — Open chapter → — so the second action is a labelled control and never a mystery second tap.

ReadsTen pictures. The most immediate of the three. ClickTurns. The link out is a separate, named target. CarefulTen cards is a card grid — the exact pattern the contents rules ban.
